Where Theta fits among your brainwaves

Your brain's electrical activity moves at different speeds depending on what you're doing. Researchers group those speeds into bands. Tap each one to see what it's usually associated with.

Theta, 4–8 Hz

Common in light meditation, daydreaming and the drowsy moments before sleep. It's often linked with relaxed alertness, creative flow and memory processing. This is the state The Genius Wave is designed to encourage.

How it's meant to work

The Genius Wave is a single 12-minute audio session. It uses sound frequencies designed to gently guide your brain toward Theta activity, an idea known as brainwave entrainment: steady rhythmic sound may encourage brain activity to fall into step with it.

There's nothing to swallow, no device to buy and nothing to install. You download the audio and play it on your phone, laptop or tablet.

The seller describes it as an at-home audio made by sound engineers and neuroscientists to support mental clarity, memory and creative thinking.

What the research actually says

Studies on sound-based entrainment show some promising but mixed results for relaxation and attention. Results vary a lot between people, and much of the research is small-scale.

A fair way to see it: a low-cost, low-risk daily calm-and-focus routine worth testing on yourself, not a guaranteed brain upgrade. The 90-day guarantee gives you room to find out.
